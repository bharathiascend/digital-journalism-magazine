   It's been a long time since I properly sat at my dusty desk and started to physically take a pen and write. I have been avoiding writing since months now, and it is clearly showing and manifesting in different ways, horrible to be specific.

A few weeks ago, I connected with my childhood friend. It was just a casual small talk, but it made me realise how much we let down our true selves. The creative self. He was a good artist who used to write fictional stories, and the characters which he used to sketch were truly amazing to see.

Unfortunately, I never got to know those characters. What are their stories? Were they a full-blown rebel who fought for their existence in their dystopian world filled with insanity? Did they fall in love with a woman, who they swore they would never marry, because they felt it was forbidden, or did they have a lame villain arc, which an uncultured man would agree to or something like that? I truly wish to know.

“Life happens.” That is what they say to creative people to stop them from expressing whimsy, ideas, and art as a whole. Sadly, the creatives convince the same thing to ease some of their lost passion due to societal pressures.

Is the passion truly lost, just like that? Gone forever in the endless dark void? I do not agree. The passion never truly vanished into thin air; the wonderful, ever-loving art was and is always there, right in front of us. We just happened to lose the magical eyesight to notice that wonderful glow.

I am starting to refer to these inspirations or sudden notices in something which sparks one’s creativity as ‘glow’. The glow to me is bright, and it welcomes me gently by nudging me towards it, waiting for me to notice its beauty. There were times when I saw multiple glows in one day, and sometimes, because of it, I got paralysed.

Paralysed on where to take the next step, which glow should I first attend to? What if this is not the perfect time for me to understand that particular, brightly shining, lovely glow? And by these recurring thoughts, I end up lying on the cold floor, consumed by the loud, never-ending noises and play pretend to myself that I am just taking a break from the misery, until now.

Until now, I realised, I mean, I honestly realised that a messy scribble is better than a blank page or an AI slop. I had to accept that my unstructured short essays, my sketches, my doodles or simply any art need nor be a conventionally perfect art piece which changes every person who is exposed to it, nor drive me insane with an amount of view counts, I can choose it to be the way it feels right for me and for me alone.

Yes, sometimes it can be daunting due to the unavoidable noises covered as expectations, instant gratification, unrealistic deadlines, which I purposefully set up for myself or fear of disappointment. But oddly, I started to come to the terms that it is okay to have a few selected people in our lives to whom we are scared to disappoint. Again, the people I chose are extremely dear to me, who will show me grace when I need it without me even asking.

Nevertheless, here I am, writing what once was a scattered, demanding, debatable noise in my head into something much more bearable. Heavily unstructured but bearable for me. When I write this, the noises in my head go almost as a whisper and make me want to truly appreciate the process and to trust it.

I may not be the best writer out there, and I am at peace with it because no matter what, I did not get paralysed, and the worst, I did not not sell my soul to AI just for it to write what I am thinking to myself. I am starting to refuse to use my limited growing critical thinking on an AI slop. I am showing up here to all the stages of me and me alone.

To conclude, I would like to say that, please make art. It can be whatever you loved to do as a child, filled with wonder! Writing whatever comes to your talkative (thinkative?) mind, drawing those shape houses with squares and triangles (you know exactly which house im talking about), accidentally getting the paint out of the border and using the wrong size paint brush and proceeding to curse it, playing with vibrant colourful clay by mixing all the colour and labelling it as the ‘vomit colour’, taking photographs of something so unique that you wish people can see exactly what you saw at that moment.

To my childhood friend with whom I connected recently, I hope you draw those amazing characters and write never-imagined fiction again, but this time only for you and you alone.  
Happy creating.
