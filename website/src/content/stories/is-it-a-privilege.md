When I was around 4, my mom taught me the concept of good girl, bad girl. When I was around 10, she gave me a different context to these same words. When I was 15, the meaning changed again. When I was 19, it changed again. Now that I'm almost 22, it has changed again.

I often wonder how women are always self-conscious? How do most women have great instincts?

I was always taught to tie my hair, wear clothes that did not reveal my skin, put on a bindi so that people knew what religion I was from, and recite prayers to ensure my devotion. I moved out of home when I was 14 but finally let go of the clutches only when I was 19. Or wait, maybe I still haven't been able to.

At 18-years-old, I thought only what my mom said was my world. What my dad said was the final say. What Patty said was the truth, but when I stepped out of my house, saw the world, I felt entirely betrayed. I was lied to. I was made to believe so many things that I didn't really have to. It made me so angry, but yet I was tied down with a huge term called RESPECT that I still shut up.

Just like everything else, one day I burst out. I decided that today I will speak how I want to, do what I want to, wear what I want to. I thought I would be really happy and free. I did so. But did I feel good?

This world named me, shamed me and tied me down with something heavier than I carried all this while, called GUILT.

There is a saying that goes for people who aren't given love in a spoon they learn to lick it off the knife, but I was given love in a whole damn tub - provided I always shook my head.

I slowly realized the definition of a woman and how I was manipulated from a very young age to bend and keep bending and even bend more when I couldn't take it.

Slowly, like very slowly, when I had the confidence to do what I wanted, even when I bore exceptional guilt, I went on with it. I did what I wanted to, faced what came along.

I slowly started doing what I wanted at home too. The first time they threw a tantrum, the second time they threatened, the third time they were disgusted, but the fourth time they resorted to guilt tripping. But this time I had armor. I didn't fall.

Today, the fact that I get to wear what I want is a huge deal. Looking back, I'm proud of myself, but looking around no one is happy. I realize it is a privilege. Now I'm scared that one day the good daughter in me might win over and will lose all that I fought for. PRIVILEGE?
