Asking the Class 12 student who decides their future who makes that decision for them would get you the answer "it's my choice"- but they'd also contradict that.

Somewhere between the syllabus and the coaching centre, the Class 12 student is expected to answer a question that adults take ages to figure out: what do you want to do with your life?

In a poll of CBSE Class 12 students, the first statement stood out as a confident answer. Asked what influenced their career choice the most, the largest group, 40%, said themselves, their interests and ambitions, followed by parents, then friends and classmates. Teachers and school came in last with only 10%. The institution designed to help them make career choices is the last one on their list.

But look a little closer and "my choice" turns into "my choice, within certain limits". Half the students said they have definitely faced pressure to pursue a career that is considered respected and successful by society. Marks, it appears, cast as long a shadow. Most said their scores dictate to a large degree which career options and colleges they can consider. It's interesting, then, to see how the conversation around interest ends, with the mark sheet.

Next came the entrance exams. 80% students said exams like JEE, NEET, CUET and CLAT make them feel like they have to decide far earlier than they should and half of them said they feel that strongly. It seems the school calendar doesn't cater to those who have to take a while deciding on their path. Most students also felt that CBSE students just don't have enough time to explore their options and hardly anyone said they definitely do.

What the timeline says is that they begin thinking about their options only after Class 10. Some did it in Class 10 and a number didn't begin until Class 12. But 40% respondents, already in their final year of school but still unsure of what they want to do, said they have no clue what to do.

The pressure follows them. Seven in ten feel moderate or heavy pressure to decide on careers and college options before school ends and 40% said it is "a lot". Meanwhile, almost everyone is quietly comparing everyone else. Most students admitted they compare their plans to their friends' some often and one always. In a Class 12 corridor, a friend's confident statement on their future feels like a judgment on you.

That comparison cuts the most when you're unsure of your own. Among students who felt uncertain, the next most common response wasn't that this makes them curious, but that they feel left behind. 40% said exactly that, and 30% said they feel very anxious. Only a few said that being undecided feels completely normal. Somehow, not having the answer has become a failure.

What's more interesting are what they ask for in return. Students didn't ask for more time. Only 10% did. They asked for exposure to the real world. The top two requests, each from 30%, were for more career counselling in school and more internships and work experience. Another 20% just wanted less pressure from their parents and society. They want to try their career on first before committing to it.

When put together, the poll shows students wanting to decide on their future in a system that decides for them, through marks, exams, societal expectations, and in comparison, to the person next to them. The students don't lack ambition, but they lack the space to explore before they're expected to have the answer.

Perhaps the most telling number is the smallest one. Only one in ten students said their school helps them most in choosing a career. The rest are relying on whatever tools they have, which include their friends, families - and their anxiety.

The poll surveyed CBSE Class 12 students, 60% women and 40% men.
