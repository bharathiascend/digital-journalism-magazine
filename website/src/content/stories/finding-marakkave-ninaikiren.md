The moment I started reading, Prabanjan's words echoed across my entire universe. In the opening pages, Mari writes about "diaries"; perhaps struck by the weight of those lines, every single page of this book felt like reading "selected pages" from Mari's own diary.

"Insult. Search. Love. Waiting. Compassion. Sorrow. Curiosity. Guilt. Shock. Sadness. Desire. Fear. Pain. Pity." Mari and his words transformed all these raw emotions into a blossoming garden of human experience.

If you have watched the Tamil film Pariyerum Perumal, you will undoubtedly feel the spirit of Jo and Pari's father in many of these pages. I have often heard people say, "Life is not that easy." But I had never truly understood it by reading about it, closing my eyes, or merely imagining it. Today, I feel it in my bones. Do not miss out on the small, fleeting joys of life. Never strive to forget your past, because in some mysterious way, those very memories will become the reason for your smile tomorrow. Mari makes us realize this profound truth.

In the middle of the book, through so many pages, I wept, I smiled, I felt enraged. But when I read the very last line and closed the book, my eyes welled up with tears. I sat there in silence, reflecting on every word, staring at the cover. Then, a single tear dropped. That final tear belonged entirely to Mari.

Can we really forget everything so easily just because we tell ourselves, "I want to forget" (Marakkavae Ninaikiren)? Reading Mari Selvaraj's work always forces us to confront uncomfortable societal mirrors. Just as Pari says in the film when faced with relentless humiliation and systemic walls: "You can beat me, you can push me down, but you cannot stop me from existing." This book carries that exact undying pulse. It is not just a narrative; it is a lived testament to survival. It reminds us that dignity is not something granted by others; it is something forged in the fires of our deepest struggles. This book doesn't just tell a story; it reshapes how you look at the world, leaving an indelible mark on your soul long after you turn the final page.
