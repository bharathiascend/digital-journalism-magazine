Tamil Nadu’s government fact-checking machinery has changed its name, its structure and, increasingly, the political context of the claims appearing on its social media feed. On the surface, it may look like a simple change in name and administration. But when you look at what the unit was doing earlier and what the new desk is focusing on now, the difference becomes much harder to ignore. 

The Tamil Nadu Fact Check Unit (TNFCU) was established by the DMK government in October 2023 to tackle misinformation concerning the state government. It was headed by Iyyan Karthikeyan, a fact-checking professional who had worked with YouTurn, and was designed as a dedicated system with a central task force in Chennai and information analysts across the districts. The unit was also authorised to proactively act on complaints and distribute fact-checks through platforms including Instagram, Facebook, X and YouTube. 

Its social media activity in 2025 reflected a fairly broad fact-checking brief. The TNFCU dealt with ordinary viral misinformation as well as politically sensitive claims. It debunked an AI-generated notice claiming that schools, banks and government offices would shut for a supposed national holiday. It also challenged BJP leader K. Annamalai’s claims about spending on Tamil Nadu government schools using official government figures. 

More significantly, the unit was willing to fact-check Vijay himself when he was in opposition. In September 2025, the TNFCU responded to Vijay’s statements concerning Nagapattinam, including claims about a government marine college and mangrove forests. The unit used government and institutional records to counter those claims. 

That old pattern matters because the political situation is now completely different. After TVK came to power, the TNFCU was renamed the Information Integrity Desk (IID). The single-unit structure was reorganised, and Guru Thalaiva, founder of the Online Thalapathy Fans Club and TVK’s social media head, was appointed to lead the IID. Prasanna, who was associated with the TVK IT wing, was appointed its project lead. A separate Social Media Desk was also created under the Directorate of Information and Public Relations, with TVK IT-wing coordinator Dhivya appointed Special Officer. 

But the most visible difference is not really the name. It is what the page is now talking about. The old TNFCU’s feed frequently focused on claims against the DMK government, but it also fact-checked opposition leaders, including Vijay and Annamalai. Under the TVK government,

the political misinformation being addressed is increasingly centred on Vijay and his government itself. 

Claims surrounding Vijay’s administration, government schemes, public services and incidents involving the Chief Minister have become prominent subjects of the new fact-checking operation. The IID, for example, has addressed a claim that an ambulance was diverted because Vijay was visiting an area, stating that the video being circulated actually predated the TVK government. It has also addressed misinformation surrounding government schemes and schools. 

That creates a very clear change in the character of the feed. Before TVK, the fact-checking unit was largely explaining or defending the government against misinformation, while also dealing with human-centric, scientific, environmental, and health-based information and checking claims made by opposition figures. Under TVK, the successor unit is increasingly being used to counter misinformation surrounding Vijay and the functioning of his government. 

The difference becomes even more striking because the person now heading the institution comes from Vijay’s own political-fan network. That does not prove that the IID is manufacturing facts or deliberately protecting TVK. But it does mean that the desk is no longer operating in the same political environment as the TNFCU. The person at the centre of many of the claims being checked is now also the Chief Minister whose government controls the fact-checking machinery. 

The government says the IID’s core mandate remains unchanged: identify misinformation and manipulated content, verify claims against official sources, and publish evidence-based fact checks. It has also introduced a ticket-based tracking system intended to make the process more accountable and traceable, with different response targets based on the urgency of a claim. 

But for a fact-checking institution, what it chooses to debunk matters almost as much as how it debunks it. The TNFCU’s old feed showed a unit that could challenge Vijay when he was an opposition leader. The IID’s current feed is operating in a system where claims about Vijay’s government are themselves a major part of its political fact-checking workload. 

The misinformation categories have not disappeared. Viral videos, political claims, government schemes and public services are still being checked. What has changed is the political subject at the centre of those claims. And that makes the IID’s future social media feed worth watching. 

If the new desk continues to scrutinise claims about TVK with the same intensity with which the old TNFCU scrutinised the DMK, it can establish itself as a credible successor. If its fact-checking increasingly becomes a tool for defending the image of the government in power, the change from “Fact Checking Unit” to “Information Integrity Desk” will look like more than a rebranding.
